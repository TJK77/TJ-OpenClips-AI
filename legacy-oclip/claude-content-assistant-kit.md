# Claude Content Assistant Kit

Everything you need to set up the content assistant from the tutorial in one file: a content brief template, a voice guide builder, the full project instructions, 15 prompts for ideas, drafts, edits, repurposing and planning, a publish check, a weekly routine and a monthly review.

Replace every [bracket] with your own details. Claude drafts and edits. You supply the real stories, check the facts and publish.

---

## 1. Five rules before you start

1. **Real stories only.** Where a real example belongs, Claude writes [YOUR STORY] and you fill it in. Never let it invent a story, result, number, quote or customer.
2. **No fake reviews.** The FTC's [final rule on fake reviews](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) bans reviews and testimonials that claim to come from someone who does not exist, such as AI-generated fake reviews. Never ask Claude for a testimonial.
3. **Read and check everything.** Google's [guidance on AI-generated content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) says to fact-check and review AI-generated content before publishing, including titles, meta descriptions and image alt text.
4. **Small and useful beats big and generic.** Google's spam policy targets large amounts of content that adds no value. A few pieces a week with your own examples is the safer plan.
5. **You publish.** Claude drafts. You edit, approve and post.

## 2. Content brief template (save as content-brief.txt)

**Who I write for:** [one sentence, including their level, for example complete beginners]

**What I sell or offer:** [one sentence]

**Three content pillars:** [1] [2] [3]

**Where I post:** [two places, for example LinkedIn and Instagram]

**Goal for the next three months:** [one goal, for example 20 enquiries from posts]

**Real stories I can tell:** [five short true moments from my work]

**Facts I can state and prove:** [prices, results, years of experience, only what is true]

**Never:** invent a story, result or customer quote. Publish a draft I have not read.

## 3. Voice guide builder

Collect three things you wrote that sound like you, then paste them under this prompt in a chat in your project.

```
Here are three things I wrote. Study how I write, then give me a voice guide I can reuse:
1. My sentence length and rhythm.
2. Words and phrases I use often.
3. Words I never use.
4. How I open and how I close.
5. Five rules, written as do and do not.
Quote a short example from my writing for each rule. Do not invent anything I did not write.

[paste sample 1]
[paste sample 2]
[paste sample 3]
```

**If you have no samples, fill this in by hand:**

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| [short sentences] | [long warm-up openers] |
| [plain words] | [hype words such as "game-changing"] |
| [start with the point] | [exclamation marks] |
| [one real example per post] | [invented stats] |
| [end with one small step] | [vague calls to action] |

Delete any rule that does not sound like you, save it as voice-guide.txt and add it to the project.

## 4. Project instructions (paste into "Set project instructions")

```
You are my content assistant for [business name].

Rules:
1. Write in my voice using voice-guide.txt. If a draft does not sound like the guide, rewrite it.
2. Use only facts from content-brief.txt and what I paste in the chat. Never invent a story, result, number, quote, student or review.
3. Where a real example belongs, write [YOUR STORY: what to tell] instead of making one up.
4. One idea per piece. Start with the point, not a warm-up.
5. Plain words. No hype, no filler, no em dashes, no exclamation marks.
6. After every draft, list the claims that need a source or my check.
7. Never write a testimonial, review or customer quote.
8. Drafts only. I read, edit and publish myself.
```

## 5. Fifteen prompts

**Ideas (3)**

**1. Ideas from real questions**

> Here are real questions my customers asked this month: [paste 8 to 10]. Give me 10 content ideas, each tied to one question and one of my three pillars. For each: a working title, the one point, and the real example I would need to supply. Rank by usefulness to a beginner. Do not repeat a topic.

**2. Pillar gap check**

> Read my last [ten] post titles: [paste]. Which of my three pillars did I neglect? Suggest five ideas for it, each tied to a real question I could ask my customers.

**3. Myths and mistakes**

> List five common mistakes beginners make in [topic] that I could correct in a post. For each, tell me what real example from my work I should add. Do not state any statistic.

**Drafting (4)**

**4. Short post**

> Write a 250-word post for idea [number]: "[title]". Audience: [who]. Use the voice guide. Leave [YOUR STORY] where a real example belongs. End with one small step the reader can take today.

**5. LinkedIn post**

> Write a LinkedIn post of about 120 words on [idea]. A personal opener, three short lines, one question at the end. Use my voice guide. Leave [YOUR STORY] for the personal part.

**6. Instagram caption**

> Write an Instagram caption of about 60 words on [idea]. Start with the point, not a hook line. At most two hashtags. Use my voice guide.

**7. Email**

> Write a short email on [idea]: a subject line under 50 characters and four sentences. One idea, one small ask. Use my voice guide. No exclamation marks.

**Editing (3)**

**8. Edit pass**

> Edit pass on the draft below. 1. Cut filler and any sentence that says nothing. 2. Replace vague words with specifics I gave you. 3. List every claim that needs checking, and where I could check it. 4. Flag any sentence that sounds like generic AI writing and offer a plainer one. Do not add new facts. [paste]

**9. Voice check**

> Compare this draft to voice-guide.txt. List three places it does not sound like me and rewrite only those sentences. [paste]

**10. Shorter version**

> Cut this to [150] words without losing the one point or my real story. Do not add anything new. [paste]

**Repurposing (3)**

**11. One post, three versions**

> Turn this post into three versions, each making the same one point: a LinkedIn post of about 120 words, an Instagram caption of about 60 words, and a short email. Keep my real story in all three. Do not add any new fact. List anything that needs my check. [paste]

**12. Thread or carousel outline**

> Turn this post into a six-slide outline: one point per slide, under 20 words each. Do not add new facts. [paste]

**13. Reply to a comment**

> Someone commented: "[comment]". Write a warm two-sentence reply in my voice that answers the question using only facts from my brief. If you cannot answer from my brief, tell me what I need to say myself.

**Planning (2)**

**14. Weekly plan**

> From these three ideas: [paste], build a one-week plan: which one goes on which day and platform, and what I need to prepare for each (real story, photo, link). No more than one piece per day.

**15. Monthly review**

> Read my weekly notes: [paste four weeks]. Tell me which pillar and which format got the most replies, which one to do less of, and three ideas for next month. Use only what I wrote in the notes.

## 6. Publish check (every piece)

- [ ] I read it out loud and it sounds like me
- [ ] Every [YOUR STORY] gap is filled with something that really happened
- [ ] I checked every claim Claude listed
- [ ] There is no invented number, quote, customer or review
- [ ] The title, description and image alt text are accurate, not stuffed
- [ ] The piece teaches one clear thing
- [ ] I know what I will say about how I use AI, if anyone asks
- [ ] I am posting it myself

---

## 7. Weekly routine (60 minutes on one batch day)

1. Paste this week's real questions and ask for ideas (prompt 1).
2. Choose three ideas where you already have a real story.
3. Draft each (prompts 4 to 7), then write your own story into each gap.
4. Run the edit pass (prompt 8) and check the claims.
5. Repurpose the best one (prompt 11).
6. Schedule across the week. No more than one piece a day.
7. Friday, five minutes: which got the most replies? Save it as voice-examples.txt and add one new rule to the project instructions.

## 8. Monthly review (20 minutes)

1. Which pillar got the most replies?
2. Which format worked best (short post, LinkedIn, caption, email)?
3. Which rule did I add most often? Move it into the voice guide.
4. Did I keep to the volume I can sustain?
5. Which real story do I still have not told?

Claude will not become your voice. It saves you the blank page, and the stories and the final say are still yours.
